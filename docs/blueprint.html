Neural Content Pipeline · System Overview

How a piece of client copy gets made

Sheet 1 of 1 · Rev. 5 (V2.2)
Updated 2026-10-05
For: non-technical review

This is a production line for writing outreach copy, such as cold emails, LinkedIn messages and landing pages, for one client at a time. You work it from a small app on your computer: pick a client, say what the piece should do, and choose the direction and the short copy yourself before anything expensive is written. Then an independent set of checks has to pass the draft before you see it. If a check fails, the system rewrites and tries again, or stops and says exactly why. It never quietly ships something that failed a check.

Where you use it

One local app with four tabs. It runs on your own machine, with the client picked in the sidebar.

Generate

A step bar shows where you are: brief, angle, short copy (optional), draft. Pick an angle, then choose the short copy or skip straight to the draft. Go back to edit the brief without losing what you typed. The finished piece sits beside its check results, copy buttons and a PDF download, with a button to add or change its short copy, and "Reuse this brief" starts the next one from the same brief.

Knowledge base

Four sub-tabs: facts to review, set-aside material, adding facts or documents, and the client profile. Verify or delete facts in bulk (delete asks first), and send a deleted or set-aside item back for review. Uploads warn before replacing a document with the same name.

Jobs

Every piece run for this client: how many finished, how many passed first time, and which check sends drafts back most often. Click a row to open a past job, or resume one that was left at the angle or short-copy step.

New client

Name the client, upload their documents, and the system drafts a style guide, tones and banned-word lists (marked DRAFT for you to review) and extracts their facts. If a drafting step fails, the client is still created and you're told what to finish by hand.

The main line

A brief goes in at the top. Finished copy comes out at the bottom. The two purple steps in the middle are yours; everything else runs by itself unless something needs a human.

Stage by stage, in plain words

In the order a piece of copy moves through them. Purple numbers are the steps where a person decides.

01

Write the brief New in V2

Pick the client and content type, then type what the piece should do. The audience, length and must-follow notes are filled in from the last brief of that type for this client, and you can edit any of them. The tone is chosen here too.

02

Readiness check

Before spending any effort, the system checks the client's facts are confirmed, sourced and dated, and that the brief has enough to work with. If not, it stops immediately instead of guessing.

03

Angle menu You choose

Drafts 2–3 cheap, genuinely different directions for the piece. You pick one before any full draft is written, so a weak direction never costs a full draft.

04

Tone

Usually one of the client's approved tones. You can also describe a new tone for this one piece, or ask the system to suggest one. A new tone is recorded on the job as a one-off and becomes a preset only if you save it.

05

Micro-copy menu Optional

Several options for each short piece of copy the content type needs: subject line, preheader and call to action for an email; opener and call to action for a LinkedIn message; title, subtitle, hook and call to action for a blog post. Any option that would break a word rule is flagged. Pick one per field or write your own. Or skip it: the draft is written without short copy, and you can add some to the finished piece (step 09).

06

Check the picked copy New in V2

Your picks go through the same banned-word and banned-term rules as the body. If one fails, the job stops before drafting, because rewriting the body can't fix a subject line.

07

Write the draft

The brief, angle, tone, your notes and the picked copy are compiled into one spec. Then the one expensive writing pass runs, using only the client's confirmed facts.

08

Five checks, run independently

Explained in the next section. This is the part that keeps the system honest.

09

Package & show

Bundles the picked copy, the draft, the other options and a compliance report. The app shows each piece of text with a copy button, the result of every check, and a PDF to download. A finished piece can get its short copy here instead: options are written to fit the draft, and your picks go through the copy checks before they join the package and PDF.

10

Approve Human step

A person reads the result and, if it's good, approves it. That closes the job and keeps the draft as a confirmed example of this client's voice for this exact format.

The checks, in detail

Nothing reaches you until it has passed every check. A check that can't run reports itself as skipped, never as a false pass.

Before drafting
Picked copy: the subject lines, openers and calls to action you chose, against the house banned-word list, stray dashes and this client's banned terms. Plain rules, zero tolerance.
Copy added to a finished piece
Same copy checks, after the fact: the picked-copy rules above, then both AI reviewers (banned framing, on-brand tone) read the finished draft with the new copy on top. The body is unchanged, so its other checks still stand. If anything fails, the package stays as it was.
On the draft
Format & wording: no banned words, no stray punctuation marks, the right length. Plain rules, zero tolerance.
This client's banned words: specific terms this client never wants used, such as a competitor's name or an overclaim.
This client's banned framing: an independent AI reviewer checks for lines that can't be reduced to one banned word: fake urgency, appealing to the wrong audience, stating an unconfirmed detail as final. It now reads the draft with your picked copy on top, the way a reader meets it.
Every fact is sourced: any number or claim in the draft must trace back to a fact that was already confirmed and dated. Nothing is invented on the spot.
On-brand, in the chosen tone: a second independent AI reviewer reads the draft cold and flags generic phrasing or clichés. It now judges against the tone you chose for this piece, including a one-off tone, and also sees the picked copy.
If a draft check fails, the draft goes back for a rewrite that is told exactly what to fix, up to two tries per check. If it fails a third time, the system stops and shows you the draft and the exact reason. If the picked copy fails, nothing is drafted: you pick different copy and run again. Copy added to a finished piece that fails a check is simply not added; pick again.

Where the facts come from

Each client has a knowledge base that feeds the "every fact is sourced" check. All of it can now be managed from the Knowledge base tab.

Claims about the clientThe founder, the format, dates, prices, results. The only kind that goes to a person for review.
Wording rules"Say this, not that." Set aside as a suggestion for the style guide or banned-word list.
AudienceWho the client sells to. Set aside for briefs; never stated as a claim.
ReferenceCompetitors, market data, general knowledge. Set aside and never used in client copy.
Not storedMeeting notes and action items, and anything personal about a named private individual.

When documents are read, every statement is labelled one of the above. Set-aside items stay on file with the reason shown, and can be sent back for review.

What changed in Rev. 5

Built and tested 2026-10-05. The checks and fact rules are unchanged; the short copy step moved from required to optional.

Changed

Micro-copy is optional

After picking an angle, choose "Choose micro-copy" as before, or "Skip micro-copy and write draft" to go straight to the body. Useful when the short copy will be written elsewhere or later.

Added

Add micro-copy to a finished piece

Every finished job, in Generate or Jobs, has an "Add micro-copy" (or "Change micro-copy") button. The options are written to fit the finished draft, including one edited by hand.

Added

Late copy is still checked

Copy added afterwards meets the same banned-word rules and both AI reviewers, reading it on top of the draft. It joins the package and PDF only if every check passes.

What changed in Rev. 4

Fixes and app improvements from 2026-10-02. The production line itself, its checks and the fact rules are unchanged.

Fixed

The Jobs tab no longer crashes after a run

After every successful run the Jobs tab failed to load. It now opens normally.

Fixed

"Stopped for a human" stays on screen

When a job stopped, the reason disappeared as soon as you clicked anything. It now stays until you move on.

Fixed

Hand-finished pieces count as finished

A piece a person finished by editing it was left out of the completion rate, so the rate read low. It now counts.

Fixed

A failed setup step no longer blocks a retry

If drafting the profile or reading documents failed while adding a client, the half-made client blocked trying again. Now the client is kept and the app says what to finish by hand.

Added

Resume a job left mid-way

A job paused at the angle or short-copy choice can be picked up again from the Jobs tab, with the tone you chose still attached.

Added

Undo a delete

A deleted fact can be sent back for review. It comes back unconfirmed, so it can't reach a draft until someone verifies it again.

Improved

An easier app to work in

A step bar, one-click angle and copy cards, a two-column brief form, the result shown beside its checks, "Edit brief" and "Reuse this brief" that keep what you typed, and buttons that say what's missing when they're greyed out.

What changed in V2

Built and tested 2026-09-28, with automated tests covering the full three-step flow.

Added

A local app instead of the command line

Generate, read and copy content, browse each client's knowledge base and job history, and set up new clients, all from one page on your own machine.

Added

You choose before the expensive part

The run now pauses twice: once to pick the angle, once to pick the short copy. Both happen before the draft is written, so a wrong direction costs a few cheap calls instead of a full draft.

Added

Short copy that fits the format, and gets checked

Before, every piece got titles, subtitles, hooks and calls to action, and none of it was checked. Now each content type gets its own fields, and the copy you pick is held to the same rules as the body.

Added

Tones beyond the presets

Describe a new tone or ask for a suggestion. The voice check judges the draft against that tone, and you can save it as a preset for next time.

Added

Job history with success rates

Each job now records the angle, tone and copy chosen. The Jobs tab shows how many finished, how many passed first time, and which check causes most rewrites.

Added

A review queue that only holds real claims

Reading documents used to queue everything checkable for review, including meeting notes and competitor research. Now only claims about the client are queued; the rest is set aside with a reason, and personal details are never kept. The first clean-up cut the queues by more than half.

Fixed

Your brief notes now reach the writer

Instructions written in a brief's notes were never passed to the writing steps, so a rule like "no em dashes" could be ignored. They now reach the angle, spec, draft and micro-copy prompts.

Rev. 2 additions (confirming a fact directly, and learning from approved work) are folded into the sections above.
